How the plan works

The ring

The ring is the whole day, from wake-up (sun) to bedtime (moon). Filled bars are naps that happened and dashed bars are suggested naps. The lighter band shows how far the day has come; the small amber dot at its end is now. Tap a logged nap, the sun or the moon on the ring to edit it, just like its row in the plan. Suggested naps show their expected start and end times along the ring (a time is left out when there is no room for it). In the middle, the countdown to the next nap or bedtime is the big number, with "Awake for" underneath: it counts from the end of the last nap or night sleep, whichever ended most recently. While a nap is running the middle switches to "Asleep for", and on the ring the nap shows as a dotted outline up to its target that fills up as she sleeps. The middle also shows the time left until the target, or how far past it she is.

A 💤 is only drawn on a nap when the bar is long enough to hold it. A very short nap is shown as a dot with a smaller 💤.

The night ring

When a night starts, the ring turns into a loading ring for the expected night: the average length of the nights from the past 8 days (10h 30m if there are none yet). The sun at the end is the expected wake-up. After that time the ring stays full and the app says it is past the usual wake-up time.

Next nap / bedtime

In the ring, while she is awake: the time until the next nap in the plan for today, or until bedtime once all planned naps are done, with its expected time right under it. If that time has passed it says "overdue by ...". It is hidden while she sleeps, and when there is not enough data for the next nap.

What "past 7 days" means

The 7 full days before today. Today never counts towards its own averages. Days with nothing logged are skipped, and a suggestion needs at least one day of data (otherwise it says "no data yet").

Nap suggestions

For each nap number (1st, 2nd, 3rd…) the app looks at the past 7 days and works out her usual awake window before that nap (from the end of the previous sleep) and her usual nap length. The next nap is placed that awake window after the previous sleep ended, so a late wake-up or a long nap moves everything after it. Recent days count more than older ones: a day's weight halves every 4 days. When there is no awake-window data yet, the usual start time is used instead. The number of naps planned is your "Target naps/day", or more if you have logged more.

Wake-up

Shown as the average wake-up time of the past 7 nights, or the real time once this morning's night sleep has ended.

Nap time target

The average total nap time per day over the past 7 days. Night sleep is not counted.

Target for the nap in progress

While she naps, the nap gets a target length. A nap before the day's last one gets her usual length for that nap. The day's last nap gets what is left of today's nap time target (at least 20 minutes), so after short naps it can catch up; but it is always long enough that the stretch from waking up to your target bedtime is at most her usual gap before bed plus 35 minutes (and at most 1.5× its usual length). So after long naps or an early morning the last nap is not cut to a catnap that would leave her awake far too long before bed. A nap beyond your target number of naps (an added nap, or one she has not had lately) is treated as a short bridge nap, so its target is at most 45 minutes (her past extra naps were mostly 25 to 45 minutes). After a nap you added, bedtime aims for your target. It is advice: the plan assumes she wakes at the target, and if she sleeps on, everything is planned from the current time.

Catching up after short naps

Naps logged today plus the suggested remaining naps are compared with the target. If they fall short, the missing time is added to the remaining naps, in proportion to how long each usually is (shown as "+20m"). A nap is stretched to at most 1.5 times its usual length (and never beyond 3 hours), because babies rarely make up a short day in one very long nap. Longer-than-usual naps do not shorten later ones. A nap that is running counts until its target (see above), or until now if it is already longer.

Adding an extra nap

"＋ Add extra nap" adds a nap to today's plan without entering any times: the plan gets one more nap, the naps still to come are re-timed and re-sized (a later nap usually gets shorter) and the new one appears. With more naps than she usually takes, the time until bedtime is shared evenly over the remaining awake windows (never longer than her usual ones), so the next nap also moves earlier. Tapping a suggested extra nap does the same: it shows as a faint pulsing bubble with a + on the ring and as "Extra nap?" with an Add button in the plan. When a nap is added, or you edit or delete a nap or bedtime and the plan changes, the ring animates into the new plan and the changed times in the list briefly turn amber. It only applies to today, on both phones. "＋ Log a nap" is for entering a nap's real times.

Extra nap

If 15 minutes or more are still missing after that, the plan suggests a short extra nap (30 to 45 minutes) halfway between the last nap and your target bedtime, a "bridge nap". It is only suggested when there are at least 75 minutes and at most 3h 30m awake on each side of it. Until you add it, it is only a suggestion: the countdown goes to bedtime, and bedtime is planned without it (usually earlier, so the last awake stretch stays within 3h 30m). Once added, the extra nap becomes the next nap and bedtime aims for its target.

Naps without data

If your target number of naps is higher than what she has had lately (say a 4th nap on 3-nap days), that nap is planned like a bridge nap: at most 45 minutes (or the usual length of the last nap that has data, if shorter), halfway between the previous nap and bedtime, so the plan and bedtime can still be worked out.

Awake window limits

Every awake window, from wake-up to the first nap, between naps, and before bedtime, is kept between 30 minutes and 3h 30m. A suggested nap never starts more than 3h 30m after the previous sleep ended. If the averages would give a longer wait, the nap is moved earlier.

Bedtime

Your target bedtime is the goal. The estimate lands on it whenever the naps allow, and it can move up to 30 minutes either way if that is needed to respect the awake limits. It also leans a little (about a third) towards the end of the last nap plus the usual gap before bedtime from recent days, always staying within those limits. If that is still not enough, the naps still to come are moved together, as little as possible, earlier or later than usual (the plan says by how much). If there are too few naps to stay under 3h 30m awake until bedtime, bedtime is moved earlier instead and the plan suggests adding a nap.

Expected vs actual

Every nap and night quietly saves what the plan expected for it (start time and length), worked out from what was logged before it. It does not matter whether you use the Start buttons or fill it in later by hand. This is used to check how accurate the plan is and to tune it over time.

Notifications

The app saves the time of the next nap or bedtime minus 30 minutes. A small job on GitHub checks about every 5 minutes (sometimes later) and sends a push to every phone with notifications turned on, saying how long is left. Each nap or bedtime is notified at most once. The notification is cancelled when the plan changes, for example when a nap starts early, and nothing is sent if the event is already less than 30 minutes away when the plan is made or if it is too late to be useful.

Symbols

~ means an estimate. ✓ means it was logged. Tap any logged row to edit it.
